Undergraduate · World models & embodied intelligence

I am Jin Li, an undergraduate in the Department of Automation at Tsinghua University. My research interests lie in world models and embodied intelligence. I work on understanding and resolving ambiguous instructions in video world models, as well as identifiable phase spaces in Hamiltonian world models.

Alongside research, I develop algorithms and robot task modules through AI and robotics competitions. In summer 2026, I served as the fieldwork group leader of Tsinghua’s MUSE delegation to the United States, exploring how AI is changing creative work, arts education and human–AI collaboration through interviews in New York and Los Angeles.

Publications & research

NeurIPS 2026Main Track · Poster · Accepted

AmbiguousWorld: Benchmarking and Resolving Ambiguous Instructions in Video World Models

Jin LiCo-second author

How can video world models follow human instructions that leave important details unstated? AmbiguousWorld combines a benchmark built on DROID with an inference-time reasoning framework. A vision–language model decomposes instructions into subgoals, explores candidate video trajectories through tree search, and uses visual feedback to prune paths that conflict with the task or physical constraints.
